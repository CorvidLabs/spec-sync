# How settled a contract is

## Intent

Not every contract deserves the same trust: a draft someone sketched this morning and a stable contract three teams depend on should not look alike. Each one carries how settled it is, and a project can make a document earn its way up rather than letting the label drift loose from the work. This is a separate question from where a change sits in its delivery, and should stay separate.

## Criteria

- **MATURITY-1**  Every contract carries how settled it is, from draft through stable to deprecated and archived.
  - **MATURITY-1.a**  I can leave contracts at a given status out of a report, so drafts and retired modules do not drown the signal.
- **MATURITY-2**  I can move a contract a step forward or back without having to remember the order myself.
  - **MATURITY-2.a**  Every contract that already qualifies can move up at once, instead of one at a time.
- **MATURITY-3**  A project can make a contract earn its promotion — a minimum score, particular sections filled in, no outstanding drift.
  - **MATURITY-3.a**  A blocked promotion names the guard that stopped it.
  - **MATURITY-3.b**  I can ask whether a promotion would pass before I attempt it.
- **MATURITY-4**  Each status change is written down with when it happened, so I can see how a module matured.
- **MATURITY-5**  A pipeline can enforce the project's maturity policy rather than trusting everyone to remember it.
  - **MATURITY-5.a**  It can require every contract to declare a status.
  - **MATURITY-5.b**  It can flag a contract that has sat in one status longer than the project allows.
- **MATURITY-6**  How settled a contract is stays separate from where a change sits in its delivery, because they answer different questions.
